Batch cooking with your own recipes

Cooking for the week is nothing new. What changes is being able to put your own family recipes into it — the ones you already cook, the ones you like, and the ones no app knows.

What a session really is

A batch session is not cooking five dishes in a row. It is looking for what those dishes share: onions chopped once, meat seared in the same pan, pasta water reused. Fewer actions, less washing up, less time standing.

Why your own recipes are the real subject

Most apps build the plan from their own catalogue: handy, but it makes you cook their recipes. What we actually eat is three or four family dishes, two or three Wednesday standbys, and leftovers. A batch that will not take those leaves out the essentials.

There is a second, less visible effect: a personal recipe already written, with its ingredients and steps, enters a session without anyone having to translate anything. It is treated exactly like a catalogue recipe.

Organising a session with three questions

What are we eating this week; which actions can be done only once; in what order, so nothing goes cold. Start with what cooks longest, finish with what is eaten cold.

What Cookerize does about it

You gather several recipes — your own included — into one session: shared actions grouped, timers set, and the plan is followed in order. A personal recipe fits in perfectly.

On the free tier a session goes up to two recipes; the subscription pushes it to ten. There is no free trial.
