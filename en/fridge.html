Cooking with what is left in the fridge

The evening meal is rarely decided in the shop. It is decided in front of an open fridge, with three vegetables starting to tire and odds and ends in the cupboard. Here is how to turn that into a real meal — and what the app does for you.

Start with what is about to spoil

Before looking for an idea, look at what is most urgent: a soft vegetable, meat to use up, yesterday's leftovers. That is what decides the dish, not the other way round. A meal that rescues three foods beats a meal that opens five.

Yesterday's cooked leftovers — rice, pasta, potatoes, roast vegetables — are not waste: they are a base. A fry-up, a soup, a gratin, a warm salad are built on them in ten minutes.

Think in roles, not in recipes

A recipe stands on four roles: a base (starch, bread, a substantial vegetable), a protein (meat, fish, eggs, pulses), an aromatic (onion, garlic, spice, herb) and a fat. Almost everything you own fits one of those boxes.

It is more flexible than it sounds: pulses replace meat, potatoes replace rice, herbs make the dish. When the four roles are there, there is a meal.

Aromatics and cooking change the country

The same vegetable, protein and starch trio gives a stir-fry, a Mediterranean dish or a Sunday casserole. A lemon, a spoon of soy sauce, a little cumin: the recipe changes without buying anything.

What Cookerize does about it

You say what you have. The app suggests the complete recipes first — the ones you can cook right now without buying anything — then the ones missing the fewest ingredients.

The fridge is sorted by aisle, like a real kitchen: vegetables, meat, dairy, groceries, spices. And when you shop, ticking a line on your list walks the ingredient into the fridge: what you have is always up to date.
