Writing a recipe you can actually follow

Many family recipes never get written down because the writing looks long. It is — unless you start from the ingredients instead of the sentences.

Start from the ingredients

A recipe is first a list: what, how much, for how many people. Once that list exists, the steps are no longer a blank page: they follow from the ingredients. A chicken, an onion, a pan — you already know you will have to chop, brown, add.

The utensil decides the rest

Pan, oven, casserole, gratin dish: this is not equipment trivia, it is what determines the actions. You do not preheat a pan like an oven, you do not cover a gratin dish. Choosing the utensil is choosing the shape of the recipe.

The order of actions is not random

In a recipe notebook, the first verbs come early — preheat, chop, brown — and the last ones close the dish: season, bake, serve. Writing in that order is writing a recipe you can follow with your hands full.

What Cookerize does about it

You add your foods: each one arrives already weighed for the recipe's number of servings. You name the utensil — pan, oven, casserole — and the steps write themselves in order, with the durations and temperatures pre-filled. You correct instead of writing: the recipe is ready in a fraction of a second.

What you write stays yours: it sits with the others, gets planned, enters the fridge and goes into a batch.
