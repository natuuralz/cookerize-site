Seasonal food, according to your region

"In season" means nothing on its own. A tomato is in season in August at almost every latitude in Europe — but in the south it starts in June, and in the north it ends earlier. What matters is the region you live in.

Three zones in Europe, three in North America

Seasonality zones follow official divisions: the European Commission for European countries, NOAA for US states. Three zones in Europe — Mediterranean, temperate, Nordic — and three in the United States — north, south, west. Two people three hundred kilometres apart are not necessarily in the same one.

Why it is worth the trouble

A seasonal dish costs less and tastes better. But the main point is elsewhere: cooking what grows now avoids buying an out-of-season vegetable that will have neither the texture nor the taste you expect — a frequent cause of kitchen disappointment.

The country matters as much as the season

What people eat is not only a matter of climate. A country's cooking habits weigh just as much: what is common in Spain is not in Scotland, and an app offering the same ideas everywhere misses what makes your own food yours.

What Cookerize does about it

You set your region, and the season search takes your zone into account: the matching recipes come up, at the right time of year for you.

On top of that, foods from your country come first, which brings up the recipes that fit you. 98 countries have their own list, each established by cross-checking at least three sources.
